[TRIGGERS]

CONTENT:

Singapore has four dental care settings, each with different pricing and subsidy access.

Private dental clinics handle about 80 percent of dental care in Singapore. Fees are set by each clinic and vary. The Ministry of Health (MOH) publishes fee benchmarks as a reference for what routine cases cost in primary care private clinics. These benchmarks cover 18 common procedures and are a reference, not a price cap.

CHAS clinics are private dental clinics that participate in the Community Health Assist Scheme. Patients with CHAS Blue, CHAS Orange, Pioneer Generation (PG), or Merdeka Generation (MG) cards receive subsidies on the 18 specified procedures at these clinics.

Polyclinics are government-run clinics that provide subsidised dental services to Singapore Citizens, with deeper discounts for children under 18 and elderly aged 65 and above. Permanent Residents pay an intermediate rate; non-residents pay full rates.

Restructured hospitals and the National Dental Centre Singapore (NDCS) handle complex or specialist dental care. Subsidised access is available for Singapore Citizens with a referral letter dated before the consultation.

HOW THE MOH FEE BENCHMARKS WORK:
In August 2025, the Dental Fee Benchmarking Advisory Committee published recommended fee benchmarks for 18 common dental procedures. MOH accepted these on 1 September 2025. From 1 October 2025, CHAS dental clinics are required to display these benchmarks alongside their own price lists. The benchmarks cover routine cases in private primary care clinics, exclude GST, and are a reference — not a strict cap.

WHAT A QUOTED FEE USUALLY INCLUDES AND EXCLUDES:
A quoted procedure fee at a private clinic typically covers the procedure itself and materials directly used. It does not usually include the consultation fee, X-rays, laboratory work for crowns or dentures, follow-up visits, or re-treatment if the procedure fails. Always ask the clinic what is and is not included before agreeing to a quote.

GST HANDLING:
GST in Singapore is currently 9 percent. At private clinics, fees are usually quoted exclusive of GST — check whether GST is added at billing. At polyclinics and restructured hospitals, displayed fees for non-subsidised patients are inclusive of GST; GST is absorbed by the government for subsidised Singapore Citizen and Permanent Resident patients.

CONTENT:

Singapore has four dental care settings. Choosing the right one depends on the treatment needed, subsidy eligibility, and whether a referral is required.

PRIVATE DENTAL CLINICS:
Private clinics handle about 80 percent of dental care in Singapore. They suit routine and typical cases, walk-in access, choice of dentist, and evening or weekend appointments. Fees are referenced by the MOH 2025 benchmarks for 18 common procedures. No subsidy applies unless the clinic is also a CHAS-participating clinic. No referral is required.

CHAS CLINICS:
CHAS clinics are private dental clinics that have signed up to the Community Health Assist Scheme. The dentists are the same as private clinics; the difference is the subsidy infrastructure. They suit Singapore Citizens with CHAS Blue, CHAS Orange, Pioneer Generation, or Merdeka Generation cards. The same MOH benchmarks apply; the subsidy is deducted from the bill. From 1 October 2025, CHAS clinics must display MOH benchmarks alongside their own price lists. No referral required. Participating clinics can be found at chas.sg.

POLYCLINICS:
Polyclinics are government-run primary care clinics operated by NHG Polyclinics (north), SingHealth Polyclinics (central, east, south), and NUHS Polyclinics (west). They handle routine dental care: examinations, cleanings, fillings, extractions, and X-rays. Complex or specialist work is referred to a restructured hospital. Subsidised rates apply for Singapore Citizens; deeper subsidies for children under 18 and elderly aged 65 and above. Permanent Residents pay an intermediate rate. CHAS and Medisave are generally not used at polyclinics for routine dental procedures. No referral required for routine work.

RESTRUCTURED HOSPITALS AND NDCS:
These institutions handle specialist procedures: root canal on molars, complex crowns, implants, full dentures, impacted wisdom tooth surgery, orthodontics, periodontal surgery, and oral and maxillofacial surgery. Institutions include Alexandra Hospital, Changi General Hospital, Khoo Teck Puat Hospital, KK Women's and Children's Hospital, National Dental Centre Singapore (NDCS), National University Centre for Oral Health Singapore (NUCOH), Ng Teng Fong General Hospital, Sengkang General Hospital, and Tan Tock Seng Hospital. A referral letter is required for subsidised access. Walk-in is possible at private (non-subsidised) rates. Medisave can be used for eligible surgical procedures.

SETTING COMPARISON — NATURAL LANGUAGE FORMAT:
A private clinic provides routine work and convenience with no subsidy unless CHAS, and no referral needed.
A CHAS clinic provides routine work for CHAS, PG, or MG cardholders with subsidy on 18 procedures, and no referral needed.
A polyclinic provides routine work at the lowest cost for citizens with built-in subsidy (deeper for children and elderly), and no referral needed.
A restructured hospital or NDCS handles complex, surgical, or specialist care with subsidies available when a referral letter is provided.

PROVIDER CONTEXT — POLYCLINICS:
Polyclinics handle basic dental care: examination, scaling and polishing, simple fillings, simple extractions, X-rays, simple root canal (front teeth only), simple crowns, simple dentures (up to 5 teeth), denture repair, and simple pulpectomy. Complex cases are referred onward. No referral is needed to visit a polyclinic — patients self-book via HealthHub (eservices.healthhub.sg/appointments) or by phone. Dental services at polyclinics are by appointment only; emergency cases are seen same day. Median waiting time for a non-emergency polyclinic dental appointment is approximately 3 months (MOH, January 2025).

PROVIDER CONTEXT — NDCS (National Dental Centre Singapore):
NDCS is Singapore's flagship National Specialty Dental Centre under SingHealth, located at the Singapore General Hospital campus. It provides all 8 MOH-recognised dental specialties: Endodontics, Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Orthodontics, Paediatric Dentistry, Periodontics, Prosthodontics, Dental Public Health, and Special Care Dentistry. It handles complex and specialist cases referred from polyclinics and CHAS clinics. A referral letter is required for subsidised rates. Private patients (no referral) are seen at full private rates. Median waiting time for a non-emergency first appointment at NDCS is approximately 1 month (33 days) (MOH). Emergency dental services are available on the same day.

PROVIDER CONTEXT — NUCOHS (National University Centre for Oral Health Singapore):
NUCOHS is the second National Specialty Dental Centre, under NUHS. It operates from three locations: main campus at 9 Lower Kent Ridge Road (co-located with NUS Faculty of Dentistry), NUCOHS Dental Clinic @ Ng Teng Fong General Hospital (Jurong East), and NUCOHS Dental Clinic @ Alexandra Hospital. It provides all 8 dental specialties plus unique multidisciplinary clinics including a Dental Sleep Medicine Clinic and an Orofacial Pain Clinic. All patients are registered as private by default; only those referred by a polyclinic or CHAS clinic receive subsidised rates. Referred patients use NUHS App or email NUHS_Dental@nuhs.edu.sg for appointments.

PROVIDER CONTEXT — RESTRUCTURED HOSPITAL DENTAL DEPARTMENTS:
Hospital dental departments (e.g., Ng Teng Fong General Hospital Division of Dentistry, Tan Tock Seng Hospital, Changi General Hospital) provide the full spectrum of general and specialist dental care, including inpatient dental management. They accept referrals from polyclinics, other hospitals, the Singapore Armed Forces, schools' dental services, and private practitioners. A referral from a polyclinic or public hospital is required for subsidised patient classification. Without a referral, the patient is classified as a private patient. The fee tables in this file labelled "2024 average fees, non-subsidised patients" reflect private patient rates at these institutions; subsidised rates for referred Singapore Citizens and PRs are substantially lower.

COST PATTERNS ACROSS SETTINGS:
A routine cleaning (scaling and polishing) is almost always cheapest at a polyclinic. A CHAS clinic with subsidy can also be very affordable. The MOH benchmark for private scaling is S$35–S$60 and polishing is S$26–S$38, excluding GST.

A simple filling is similarly cheaper at a polyclinic. The MOH benchmark for a private simple filling is S$65–S$160 and complex filling is S$80–S$180, excluding GST.

A permanent crown typically costs more at a restructured hospital than at a private clinic for comparable cases. The MOH benchmark for a private permanent crown is S$750–S$1,400. Restructured hospital averages range from approximately S$804 to S$1,491 per unit before subsidy.

A dental implant is not typically cheap anywhere. Restructured hospital averages range from approximately S$4,539 to S$6,600 per unit (implant plus crown). Medisave can be used for the surgical portion.

A dental consultation is the first appointment where the dentist examines your teeth and gums, takes any X-rays needed, and proposes a treatment plan. It is almost always billed separately from the procedure — even if a procedure is done in the same visit, the consultation has its own fee.

PRIVATE CLINICS — MOH 2025 FEE BENCHMARK (excluding GST):
The MOH reference range for a routine consultation at a private primary care clinic is S$21 to S$31. Specialist consultations (periodontics, orthodontics, oral surgery) typically fall above this range.

In natural language: A consultation at a typical private dental clinic in Singapore costs S$21 to S$31 before GST, per the MOH 2025 benchmark. This is a reference range, not a price cap.

NATIONAL DENTAL CENTRE SINGAPORE (NDCS) — PRIVATE (NON-SUBSIDISED) PATIENTS:
NDCS charges for consultation depend on clinician rank and visit type. A first visit with a Senior Consultant costs S$186. A first visit with a Consultant costs S$164. A first visit with an Associate Consultant costs S$142. Repeat visits with any rank cost S$81. A sterile supplies fee of S$17 to S$18 applies at each visit as a separate line item. Private prices include GST.

WHAT IS NOT INCLUDED IN THE CONSULTATION FEE:
A consultation fee does not include X-rays taken during the visit, cleaning or any procedure performed during the visit, a sterile supplies fee at hospital settings, or specialist referral if recommended.

WHO PROVIDES CONSULTATIONS AT EACH SETTING:
At polyclinics, consultations are conducted by registered dentists and oral health therapists (OHTs). OHTs are trained and supervised health professionals who assist with basic dental procedures. Polyclinic dental consultations are by appointment only. For a new consultation at a polyclinic, the patient does not need a referral and can self-book via HealthHub.
At CHAS dental clinics (private clinics in the CHAS scheme), consultations are conducted by registered dentists. No referral is needed. CHAS subsidy applies to the consultation fee for eligible cardholders.
At NDCS, consultations are conducted by dental specialists and specialist trainees (residents), supervised by senior clinicians. Patients requiring subsidised access must present a referral letter from a polyclinic or CHAS clinic. Private patients (without referral) pay the private rates listed in this chunk.
At NUCOHS, the same specialist-level consultation applies. All patients are treated as private by default unless referred from a polyclinic or CHAS clinic. NUCOHS is contactable at +65 6716 2233 or NUHS_Dental@nuhs.edu.sg for appointment queries.

CONTENT:

Professional dental cleaning combines scaling (removal of plaque and calculus from tooth surfaces, particularly at and below the gum line) and polishing (smoothing tooth surfaces and removing surface stains). They are usually billed as separate line items.

PRIVATE CLINICS — MOH 2025 FEE BENCHMARK (excluding GST):
The MOH reference range for scaling at a private primary care clinic is S$35 to S$60. For polishing it is S$26 to S$38. A combined scaling and polishing visit at a private clinic therefore typically falls in the S$61 to S$98 range before GST, before any consultation fee, and before any X-rays. The benchmark is a reference, not a strict cap. Fees may be higher for heavy calculus build-up, gum disease, or complicating factors.

MEDISAVE: Medisave does not cover routine dental cleaning. Scaling and polishing are not surgical procedures and have no TOSP code.

WHAT IS TYPICALLY NOT INCLUDED IN A CLEANING FEE:
A scaling and polishing fee does not automatically include the consultation fee for the visit, X-rays if taken, or topical fluoride application (often offered as an add-on).

WHEN DEEPER CLEANING IS NEEDED:
If moderate or advanced gum disease is present, the dentist may recommend scaling and root planing (sometimes called deep cleaning), which goes below the gum line into periodontal pockets. This is a separate procedure not covered under the standard CHAS scaling subsidy and is typically performed across two or more visits.

WHO PROVIDES SCALING AND POLISHING AT EACH SETTING:
At polyclinics, scaling and polishing is provided by dentists and oral health therapists (OHTs). It is a standard polyclinic dental service. No referral is needed.
At CHAS dental clinics, scaling and polishing is a routine service. CHAS subsidy applies per procedure.
At NDCS and NUCOHS (National Specialty Dental Centres), routine scaling and polishing is not the primary focus. Oral health therapists at NDCS perform scaling and polishing for patients referred by specialists within NDCS (e.g. for periodontal or orthodontic cases). Patients needing only routine cleaning are better served at polyclinics or CHAS clinics.

CONTENT:

Dental X-rays come in several types:
Periapical X-rays show single-tooth detail including the root and surrounding bone.
Bitewing X-rays image back teeth on both arches in one image and are useful for spotting cavities between teeth.
Occlusal X-rays provide a broader view of the floor of the mouth or palate.
Orthopantomogram (OPG or panoramic X-ray) provides a full-mouth single image and is standard for treatment planning, wisdom tooth assessment, and implant planning.

PRIVATE CLINICS — MOH 2025 FEE BENCHMARK (excluding GST):
A periapical, bitewing, or occlusal X-ray costs S$16 to S$33 before GST. An OPG (panoramic X-ray) costs S$65 to S$104 before GST.

Whether a procedure is claimable is determined by the Table of Surgical Procedures (TOSP), a Ministry of Health master list of procedures eligible for Medisave and MediShield Life claims. If a dental procedure has a TOSP code it is in principle Medisave-claimable; if it does not, it is not. The dollar amount claimable is determined by the procedure's TOSP Table classification and CPF Board scheme rules.

MEDISAVE ELIGIBILITY:
To use Medisave for dental, you must be a Singapore Citizen or Permanent Resident, must be at least 18 years old, and must have sufficient Medisave balance. - Periodontal surgery (gum grafting, gingivectomy, crown lengthening, guided bone regeneration): NDCS (Periodontics), NUCOHS (Periodontics), and restructured hospital dental departments.
- Surgical endodontics (apicoectomy): NDCS (Endodontics) and NUCOHS (Endodontics).
- Other oral surgery (cyst removal, biopsy, incision and drainage, alveoloplasty, embedded tooth removal): NDCS and NUCOHS Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery departments, and restructured hospital dental/oral surgery departments.
Patients must be referred from a polyclinic or public hospital to access these services at subsidised rates. Without a referral, private patient rates apply. Medisave can be used regardless of subsidised or private patient status, subject to available balance and procedure eligibility.

The Community Health Assist Scheme (CHAS) provides dental subsidies to eligible Singapore Citizens at CHAS-participating private dental clinics. Cards are issued in four tiers based on age and means-testing. CHAS subsidies were enhanced from 1 October 2025.

THE FOUR TIERS (highest to lowest subsidy):

CHAS Orange: Singapore Citizens with monthly household income per person of approximately S$2,001 to S$2,800. From 1 October 2025, CHAS Orange was expanded to cover all 18 procedures — previously it covered only 7.

WHAT CHAS DOES NOT COVER:
CHAS dental subsidies cover only the 18 procedures listed above. Treatments outside this list are not covered, including dental implants, veneers, teeth whitening, orthodontic treatment (braces or clear aligners), cosmetic procedures, periodontal surgery, and most other surgical procedures (which may be Medisave-claimable instead).

HOW TO USE CHAS:
1. Verify your CHAS card status — cards are valid for two years before renewal.
2. Find a participating CHAS dental clinic at chas.sg.
3. Present your physical or digital CHAS card at the clinic before treatment.
4. The clinic processes the claim; the subsidy is deducted from your bill.

The subsidy is per claim per procedure, not per visit. A single visit with multiple procedures generates separate subsidy claims, each subject to its own cap.

From 1 October 2025, CHAS dental clinics must display the MOH benchmarks alongside their price lists, allowing patients to see the clinic's fee, the MOH benchmark, the CHAS subsidy, and the expected co-payment before proceeding.

IMPORTANT: The CHAS subsidy is a maximum cap per claim, not a guaranteed amount. Verify eligibility at chas.sg or with the clinic's billing team. The chatbot cannot confirm individual entitlement amounts.

WHERE CHAS SUBSIDIES APPLY AND DO NOT APPLY:
CHAS dental subsidies apply only at CHAS-participating private dental clinics. They do not apply at polyclinics, NDCS, NUCOHS, or restructured hospital dental departments.
At polyclinics, built-in government subsidies apply automatically for Singapore Citizens and PRs — CHAS cards are not used at polyclinics for routine dental procedures.
At NDCS and NUCOHS (National Specialty Dental Centres), CHAS subsidies do not apply. Subsidised access at NSCs is available through the referral pathway — patients referred by polyclinics or CHAS clinics receive government-subsidised (means-tested) rates, which are separate from and independent of CHAS card subsidies.
At restructured hospital dental departments, the same applies — CHAS subsidies do not apply, but patients referred from polyclinics or public hospitals receive subsidised hospital rates.
CHAS cardholders (PG, MG, CHAS Blue, CHAS Orange) who are referred from a CHAS dental clinic to NDCS or NUCOHS for specialist care will receive government-subsidised specialist rates at the NSC — the CHAS subsidy itself does not follow the patient to the NSC, but the referral pathway enables subsidised care there.

[DOMAIN]     Dental costs and subsidies — Singapore

[TRIGGERS]

  - Can seniors use Medisave for a crown?
  - How does Flexi-MediSave work?
  - When does Flexi-MediSave for dental start?
  - How much Flexi-MediSave can I use per year?
  - Where can I use Flexi-MediSave?

CONTENT:

Flexi-MediSave is a Medisave-funded dental enhancement that takes effect from mid-2026. It allows Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents aged 60 and above to use Medisave for two specific non-surgical dental procedures that are not covered by standard Medisave. This is a meaningful change for seniors managing costs of crowns and root canals.

ELIGIBILITY:
You must be a Singapore Citizen or Permanent Resident, aged 60 years or above, with sufficient Medisave balance. Eligibility for Flexi-MediSave is independent of CHAS card status — a senior who does not hold a CHAS card can still use Flexi-MediSave at a CHAS clinic.

WHAT IT COVERS:
Up to S$400 per year can be drawn from Medisave under Flexi-MediSave for dental. This S$400 is an annual cap that applies across both covered procedure categories combined:
- Permanent Crowns
- Root Canal Treatments

WHERE IT CAN BE USED:
Flexi-MediSave is available at CHAS dental clinics and public healthcare institutions (restructured hospitals, NDCS, NUCOH). It does not apply at non-CHAS private clinics.

ANNUAL CAP TIMING:
The S$400 limit is per calendar year, not per procedure. If a crown draws the full S$400 in one year, a root canal in the same year cannot draw any additional Flexi-MediSave until the following year. For treatment plans involving multiple crowns or root canals, spreading procedures across calendar years maximises Flexi-MediSave use.

PRACTICAL EXAMPLE FOR A SENIOR WITH A PG OR MG CARD NEEDING A CROWN:
Step 1: MOH benchmark for a private permanent crown is S$750 to S$1,400 before GST.
Step 2: PG subsidy at the CHAS clinic deducts up to S$625 from the bill.
Step 3: Flexi-MediSave then offsets up to S$400 of the remaining balance.
Step 4: The patient pays the remaining out-of-pocket balance in cash.
This combination of CHAS subsidy plus Flexi-MediSave can substantially reduce the cost of a crown for qualifying seniors.

WHAT FLEXI-MEDISAVE DOES NOT COVER:
Flexi-MediSave does not cover procedures other than permanent crowns and root canal treatments. It does not apply at non-CHAS private clinics. It does not apply for patients younger than 60.

CHAS CLINIC ACCREDITATION:
As part of the Flexi-MediSave roll-out, all CHAS dental clinics are required to be Medisave-accredited and onboard Flexi-MediSave by 31 December 2026. CHAS clinics that are not accredited by that date will be offboarded from the CHAS scheme. In practice, most CHAS clinics will accept Flexi-MediSave from mid-2026 onward. If a clinic does not yet accept it, ask before booking.

IMPORTANT: The chatbot cannot confirm individual Medisave balance or annual usage. Verify via cpf.gov.sg or the clinic before treatment.

WHERE FLEXI-MEDISAVE CAN BE USED — PROVIDER DETAILS:
Flexi-MediSave is accepted at CHAS dental clinics and public healthcare institutions. Public healthcare institutions in this context include: polyclinics (for the procedures they provide), NDCS, NUCOHS, and restructured hospital dental departments. It does not apply at non-CHAS private dental clinics.
For crowns: Flexi-MediSave can be used at CHAS clinics (for crowns covered under CHAS) and at public institutions including NDCS, NUCOHS, and restructured hospital dental departments. This means a senior aged 60+ getting a crown at NDCS or NUCOHS as a referred subsidised patient can additionally claim up to S$400/year via Flexi-MediSave on top of any hospital subsidy.
For root canal treatment: The same applies. Flexi-MediSave can be used at CHAS clinics and public institutions (NDCS, NUCOHS, restructured hospitals) for root canal treatment in patients aged 60+.

CONTENT:

This chunk summarises eligibility for each dental subsidy scheme in Singapore to help patients identify which schemes apply to them.

PIONEER GENERATION (PG) PACKAGE:
Who qualifies: Singapore Citizens born on or before 31 December 1949, who became Singapore Citizens by 31 December 1986. The PG card is issued automatically — no application needed. If you believe you are eligible but have not received a card, contact the Pioneer Generation Office or visit pioneers.gov.sg.
Subsidy level: Highest tier. PG subsidies are the largest across all 18 CHAS-covered dental procedures.
Where it applies: CHAS dental clinics only.

MERDEKA GENERATION (MG) PACKAGE:
Who qualifies: Singapore Citizens born between 1 January 1950 and 31 December 1959, who became Singapore Citizens by 31 December 1996, and who are not eligible for the Pioneer Generation Package. The MG card is issued automatically. For more information visit merdeka.gov.sg.
Subsidy level: Second highest, slightly below PG across all 18 procedures.
Where it applies: CHAS dental clinics only.

CHAS BLUE:
Who qualifies: Singapore Citizens with monthly household income per person of approximately S$1,200 to S$2,000 (or Annual Value of home approximately S$13,001 to S$21,000 for households with no income). Final eligibility determined by the Agency for Integrated Care (AIC). Apply or check eligibility at chas.sg or via Healthhub.
Subsidy level: Third tier; covers all 18 dental procedures.
Where it applies: CHAS dental clinics only.

CHAS ORANGE:
Who qualifies: Singapore Citizens with monthly household income per person of approximately S$2,001 to S$2,800 (or Annual Value of home approximately S$21,001 to S$31,000 for households with no income). Final eligibility determined by the AIC. Apply or check at chas.sg or via Healthhub.
Subsidy level: Lowest CHAS tier; covers all 18 dental procedures from 1 October 2025 (coverage of the 10 basic and preventive procedures was new from that date).
Where it applies: CHAS dental clinics only.

POLYCLINIC SUBSIDY (built-in, for routine care):
Who qualifies: Singapore Citizens receive subsidised rates automatically at government polyclinics. Deeper subsidies apply for children under 18 and elderly aged 65 and above. Permanent Residents receive an intermediate subsidised rate. Non-residents pay full rates.
No application is needed for polyclinic subsidies — they are applied automatically based on identity documents.
Note: CHAS and Medisave are generally not used at polyclinics for routine dental procedures.

MEDISAVE (for surgical procedures):
Who qualifies: Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents aged 18 and above with sufficient Medisave balance. Covers surgical dental procedures with a TOSP code. Does not cover routine non-surgical work.

FLEXI-MEDISAVE (from mid-2026, for crowns and root canals only):
Who qualifies: Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents aged 60 and above. Available at CHAS dental clinics and public healthcare institutions. Independent of CHAS card status.

PERMANENT RESIDENTS:
Permanent Residents are not eligible for CHAS, Pioneer Generation, or Merdeka Generation subsidies. They receive intermediate subsidised rates at polyclinics (between citizen and non-resident rates), and they can use Medisave for eligible surgical procedures.

NON-RESIDENTS:
Non-residents pay full (non-subsidised) rates at all dental settings, including polyclinics and public hospitals. No subsidy schemes apply.

HOW TO CHECK YOUR CHAS TIER:
Check your physical CHAS card — the card colour and label indicate the tier. Alternatively, check via the Healthhub app or at chas.sg. Cards must be renewed every two years.
