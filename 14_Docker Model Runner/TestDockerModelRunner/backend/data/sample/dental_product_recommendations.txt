WHAT DENTAL FLOSS IS:
Dental floss is a thin nylon or PTFE thread used to remove plaque and food debris from between teeth — surfaces that a toothbrush cannot reach. For users with tightly spaced teeth and healthy gums, it is often the right interdental cleaning choice. For users with wider gaps, gum recession, crowns, bridges, or implants, an interdental brush or water flosser is usually more effective.

TYPES OF DENTAL FLOSS:

Waxed floss: coated in wax to slide more easily between tightly spaced teeth. The default choice for most adults. Available everywhere in Singapore.

Unwaxed floss: slightly thinner; some users prefer the texture. Shreds more easily on rough fillings or crowns.

PTFE (Teflon-based) floss: slides smoothly even between very tight contacts; less likely to shred. Generally more expensive.

Dental tape: a wider, ribbon-like floss that can feel gentler on the gums; suitable for slightly wider gaps.

Floss picks (disposable Y- or F-shaped handles with a short floss segment): easier to handle than spool floss, particularly for users with limited dexterity. Less effective than spool floss for getting into the C-shape around each tooth, but better than not flossing at all.

Super floss / orthodontic floss: has a stiff threader at one end and a spongy section. Designed for braces, bridges, and around implants.

HOW TO FLOSS:
1. Take 30 to 45 cm of floss. Wrap most around the middle finger of one hand, a small amount around the other.
2. Hold a 2 to 3 cm working section between thumbs and index fingers.
3. Slide gently between two teeth using a side-to-side sawing motion — do not snap it down.
4. Curve the floss into a C-shape against one tooth, slide up and down slightly under the gum line. Repeat against the neighbouring tooth.
5. Use a fresh section of floss for each gap to avoid transferring bacteria.
6. Cover every gap including the back of the last molar. A full pass takes 60 to 90 seconds.

FLOSS BEFORE BRUSHING: most people find it more practical to floss last thing at night before brushing, so any loosened debris is then swept away during brushing.

COMMON MISTAKES:
Snapping floss down hard between teeth (can cut the gum). Using one short section for the whole mouth. Sawing back and forth at the gum line rather than wrapping around each tooth. Stopping because gums bleed — bleeding is from inflammation, not injury; consistent daily flossing is what resolves it within one to two weeks.

WHEN TO SWITCH TOOLS:
If floss shreds repeatedly on the same tooth, there may be a rough filling or crown edge needing attention from a dentist. If bleeding does not decrease after three weeks, see a dentist — the gum issue may be beyond simple gingivitis. If flossing simply will not be sustained daily, switch to interdental brushes or a water flosser.

WHAT INTERDENTAL BRUSHES ARE:
Interdental brushes are small wire brushes shaped like miniature bottle brushes, sized to fit between specific pairs of teeth. For many users — particularly those with gum recession, wider spaces, crowns, bridges, or implants — they remove more plaque per minute than dental floss and are often easier to use daily.

SIZING — THE MOST IMPORTANT DETAIL:
The brush must fit snugly between teeth without forcing. Most adults need two or three different sizes for different parts of the mouth. Gaps between front teeth are usually smaller than those between back teeth.

Sizing approach: buy a variety or mixed pack first. Try each size in different gaps. The right brush goes in with gentle resistance — if it requires real force, it is too big. Note which size works where and repeat those purchases.

TePe Colour-Code Guide (most used in Singapore clinics):
Pink = 0.4 mm (very small, front teeth). Orange = 0.45 mm. Red = 0.5 mm. Blue = 0.6 mm (medium, common for adults). Yellow = 0.7 mm. Green = 0.8 mm. Purple = 1.1 mm. Grey = 1.3 mm. Black = 1.5 mm (large gaps, recession, around implants).

HOW TO USE:
1. Insert gently between two teeth, angled slightly toward the gum line. Do not push if it does not go in.
2. Push through and back out a few times. The bristles sweep both adjacent tooth surfaces.
3. No toothpaste is needed (some users dip in mouthwash).
4. Rinse the brush under running water after each gap.
5. Replace when bristles splay or look matted — typically every one to two weeks.
A full pass through the mouth takes 60 to 90 seconds.

CARE: rinse and shake dry after each use. Store upright in a clean dry place. Do not share.

WHEN FLOSS IS BETTER: very tightly spaced teeth where even the smallest brush does not fit; all-natural teeth with no recession. In practice, many users benefit from using both — floss for tightly spaced front areas, interdental brushes for back teeth.

WHAT A WATER FLOSSER IS:
A water flosser (oral irrigator) directs a pulsating jet of water between teeth and along the gum line. It removes loose plaque, food debris, and bacteria — particularly useful around bridges, implants, and braces where floss and interdental brushes are harder to manoeuvre.

A water flosser is not a substitute for brushing. It is a reasonable alternative or complement to floss for most users. For users with tight tooth contacts and healthy gums, traditional floss is more thorough at the actual contact point. For users with bridges, implants, or wider gaps, water flossing is often equally effective or better.

TYPES:
Countertop models: sit on the counter, plug into mains, larger reservoir (less refilling), wider pressure range, more attachments. Best for daily home use.
Cordless/rechargeable models: handheld, smaller reservoir, portable. Slightly less powerful than countertop. Best for travel or small bathrooms.

FEATURES WORTH HAVING:
- Multiple pressure settings. Start at the lowest setting; always build up gradually. High pressure can damage gum tissue.
- A periodontal tip (for users with gum disease, with a soft rubber tip that enters slightly under the gum line) or an orthodontic tip (bristles for users with braces).
- Reservoir capacity of at least 600 ml for a countertop model so the whole mouth can be cleaned without refilling.

HOW TO USE:
1. Fill the reservoir with lukewarm water (cold water is uncomfortable, especially with gum recession).
2. Choose the tip: standard for most users; periodontal for gum pockets; orthodontic for braces.
3. Lean over the sink. Place the tip just at the gum line between two teeth, angled slightly toward the gum.
4. Turn on at the lowest pressure setting. Move slowly along the arch, pausing a couple of seconds at each gap.
5. A full pass takes 60 to 90 seconds.
Use once daily, ideally before brushing so loosened debris is then brushed away.

CAUTIONS: Do not use at maximum pressure if you have gum recession or bleeding gums. If you have a recent extraction or surgical site, follow your dentist's specific guidance before using a water flosser near that area.

WHY ELECTRIC TOOTHBRUSHES MAKE A DIFFERENCE:
Independent reviews consistently show that powered brushes remove more plaque than manual brushes, with the greatest benefit for people with high plaque accumulation, limited dexterity, or a habit of pressing too hard. A manual brush used well outperforms an electric brush used poorly — technique and duration matter most. But for most adults, switching to an electric brush with a timer and pressure sensor is one of the highest-impact practical changes available.

THE TWO MAIN TYPES:

Oscillating-rotating: the round brush head rotates back and forth in short bursts, often pulsing to disrupt plaque. The most clinically validated category. Best for users new to electric brushing; users with crowded teeth where the small round head reaches each tooth individually; users wanting the strongest evidence base.

Sonic: the brush head vibrates at very high frequency in a small side-to-side motion. Vibration creates fluid dynamics that help disrupt plaque slightly beyond where bristles directly touch. Best for users who prefer a more familiar back-and-forth feel; users with gum recession or sensitivity; users with bridges or implants where fluid dynamics help clean around restorations.

ESSENTIAL FEATURES:
- Two-minute built-in timer with 30-second quadrant prompts.
- Pressure sensor that warns visibly when pressing too hard — particularly useful for users with gum recession.
- Sensitive mode for users with gum recession or general sensitivity.
- Replaceable brush head — replace every three months.

WHY SOFT BRISTLES ALWAYS:
Medium and hard bristles offer no cleaning benefit and accelerate gum recession and enamel wear. Soft or extra-soft bristles, used with correct Modified Bass technique for two minutes, remove plaque effectively without damaging gum tissue. This applies to every adult, every time — there is no situation in which a medium or hard bristle brush is the right choice for a patient.

WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
- Soft or extra-soft bristles only.
- A small to compact head that can reach the back of the mouth and individual teeth.
- A non-slip handle comfortable to grip for two minutes.
- Tapered or rounded bristle tips, gentle on the gum margin.

REPLACEMENT: every three months, or sooner if bristles splay outwards. Splayed bristles indicate the brush is no longer cleaning effectively — and often that too much pressure is being applied.

THE ONLY THING THAT MATTERS IN TOOTHPASTE — FLUORIDE:
A fluoride toothpaste at 1,000 to 1,500 ppm is the baseline cavity-prevention tool for all adults at typical risk. The clinical evidence for fluoride is extremely strong — it is the single most well-evidenced cavity-prevention measure in dentistry. Brand differences at this concentration are small and unlikely to translate to noticeable differences in oral health. Choose one you will use consistently, twice a day.

WHAT TO LOOK FOR ON THE PACK:
- 1,000 to 1,500 ppm fluoride. This must appear on the pack. Products marketed as "natural" or "fluoride-free" do not meet this.
- Sodium fluoride (NaF) or sodium monofluorophosphate (MFP) or stannous fluoride as the active ingredient. All work.

HOW TO USE:
Pea-sized amount. Twice daily — morning and last thing at night. Spit but do not rinse with water — leaving the residual film on the teeth significantly extends fluoride contact time. Do not eat or drink for 30 minutes after brushing, especially at night.

ON WHITENING, CHARCOAL, AND NATURAL TOOTHPASTES:
Whitening toothpastes work by mechanical abrasion; they can be more abrasive and are not ideal for users with gum recession or sensitivity. Charcoal toothpastes are abrasive, lack robust evidence of benefit, and most do not contain fluoride — their use trades cavity protection for unproven benefit. Not recommended. "Natural" or fluoride-free toothpastes typically lack fluoride, the most evidenced cavity-prevention ingredient.

WHAT DESENSITISING TOOTHPASTE DOES:
A desensitising toothpaste contains fluoride (same cavity protection as standard toothpaste) plus additional active ingredients that reduce sensitivity from exposed dentine — the inner tooth layer exposed when gum recession reveals root surfaces. It is an additional layer of protection, not a cure for the underlying cause.

Mild root sensitivity from gum recession often responds well. Sensitivity from a cavity, cracked tooth, exposed nerve, or recent dental work needs clinical attention — toothpaste alone will not address it.

TWO MAIN ACTIVE-INGREDIENT APPROACHES:
- Potassium nitrate (e.g. Sensodyne Gum Care, Sensodyne Complete Protection): calms the nerve response inside the tooth by reducing nerve excitability. Effect builds over two to four weeks of consistent twice-daily use.
- Stannous fluoride or arginine-calcium-carbonate (e.g. Sensodyne Rapid Relief, Colgate Sensitive Pro-Relief): physically blocks dentine tubules. Some formulations claim faster relief within a few days. Both approaches work well for most users.

HOW TO USE:
Use twice a day in place of regular toothpaste — not as an addition. Be patient: most desensitising toothpastes take two to four weeks of consistent twice-daily use before the effect peaks. Stopping after a few days because it is "not working" is the most common reason patients do not see benefit. Continue indefinitely if it works. For an extra-targeted effect: after brushing, put a small amount on a clean fingertip and rub into the sensitive root surface, leaving it on overnight.

WHEN TO SEE A DENTIST: if sensitivity persists or worsens after four weeks of consistent use, see a dentist. A professional fluoride varnish application can provide faster and more durable relief. Severe localised sensitivity from gum recession may warrant a gum graft.

THREE TYPES OF ANTISEPTIC MOUTHWASH:

Chlorhexidine (0.12% or 0.2%):
The most clinically potent antiseptic mouthwash. Highly effective at reducing plaque and gum inflammation. Short-term use only — typically two to four weeks at a time, often prescribed after periodontal treatment, oral surgery, or to break a plaque cycle. Do not use within 30 minutes of brushing — chlorhexidine is inactivated by sodium lauryl sulfate in most toothpastes. Essential-oil mouthwashes (e.g. Listerine-style):

A milder antiseptic for daily use. Modest evidence for plaque reduction. Usually well-tolerated. Often combined with fluoride in over-the-counter rinses (e.g. Colgate Plax).

A replacement for brushing or interdental cleaning. A treatment for gum disease on its own. A daily long-term necessity for users with healthy gums.

WHAT A NIGHT GUARD IS:
A night guard (occlusal splint) is a thin custom-fitted plastic appliance worn over the upper or lower teeth at night to protect them from grinding (bruxism) or clenching forces. It absorbs and distributes grinding forces so the wear happens to the plastic rather than enamel, and it protects existing crowns, veneers, and fillings from excessive bite forces.

WHAT A NIGHT GUARD DOES NOT DO:
It does not stop grinding from happening — it is a protective device, not a cure for bruxism. It does not fix gum recession, cavities, or worn fillings. Stress, sleep position, and sleep disorders that drive bruxism are addressed separately. Pain, sudden bite changes, or persistent jaw stiffness should be clinically assessed by a dentist before assuming bruxism is the only cause.

CUSTOM-FITTED (DENTIST-MADE) vs OVER-THE-COUNTER:

Custom-fitted (dentist-made):
The dentist takes impressions or digital scans of your teeth; the guard is made at a dental laboratory to fit exactly. Best fit, most durable (five to ten years with good care), most comfortable, bite-adjusted by the dentist. Recommended for established bruxism, extensive dental work, or persistent jaw symptoms. Cost in Singapore: typically several hundred dollars (S$300–S$700+) depending on clinic and material.

Over-the-counter boil-and-bite:
Softened in hot water and bitten into to shape. Affordable (S$10–S$40), available at most Singapore pharmacies. Reasonable for short-term use while waiting for a custom guard, or as a trial. Less durable (often needs replacing within months), less comfortable, bite not adjusted. Not appropriate for established bruxism, multiple crowns, or persistent symptoms. A poorly fitting guard can sometimes worsen jaw symptoms.

Stock guards (one-size-fits-all):
Sometimes sold in pharmacies. Generally not recommended — fit is poor and most users abandon them quickly.
