Oral Link (company name: Oral Link Pte Ltd) is a Singapore-based health technology company specialising in AI-powered dental screening. It is not a dental clinic itself — it is a digital health platform that connects patients with a network of trusted partner dental clinics.

MISSION:
Oral Link's mission is to enhance oral health across Singapore through innovative, patient-focused technology. The company uses AI-powered solutions to make oral healthcare accessible, affordable, and efficient for everyone — including seniors, individuals with limited mobility, and populations who do not regularly visit a dentist.

WHAT ORAL LINK PROVIDES:
Oral Link provides an AI dental screening service that allows patients to take photos of their own teeth using a smartphone, receive an AI-generated risk report within hours, and then connect with a partner dental clinic for follow-up treatment. 50 percent of children in Singapore have cavities by kindergarten. Three in four adults have untreated gum disease or tooth decay. Most dental check-ups in Singapore happen only during emergencies. Oral diseases cost US$710 billion globally each year and Singapore's costs are rising. Oral Link was founded to address these gaps through preventive, technology-driven care.

Oral Link's dental screening service works in four steps.

STEP 1 — SNAP AND SUBMIT:
Visit the Oral Link platform and take 5 photos of your teeth using your own smartphone. The platform provides guided instructions so you know exactly which angles to capture. No special equipment is needed — just your phone.

STEP 2 — AI SCREENING:
Once you submit your photos, Oral Link's AI instantly analyses the images and generates a personalised dental risk report. The AI is capable of detecting conditions including dental cavities, gum inflammation, gum recession, plaque build-up, and cracked teeth.

STEP 3 — RECEIVE YOUR REPORT:
You receive a clear report with findings and treatment guidance. The report highlights potential concerns — from cavities and gum issues to early warning signs of more serious conditions — and is accessible from your device. Reports are typically returned within hours (the service targets a 10-minute report turnaround for standard cases).

STEP 4 — COMPLETE CARE:
Using the report, you choose a trusted partner dental clinic from the Oral Link network and book an appointment for in-person assessment and treatment. The attending dentist reviews the AI findings, performs a clinical examination, and finalises a personalised treatment plan.

WHAT THE REPORT DOES AND DOES NOT DO:
The AI report is a screening tool — it flags areas of concern and risk levels. It is not a clinical diagnosis. Final diagnosis and any treatment decisions are made by a qualified dentist during the in-person visit. The report helps patients and dentists prioritise attention efficiently.

Oral Link's AI screening is designed to detect the following dental conditions from smartphone photos:

DENTAL CAVITIES (TOOTH DECAY):
The AI analyses tooth surfaces for signs of decay — dark spots, shadows between teeth, and surface breakdown that indicate cavities. Early-stage cavities that a patient might not yet feel pain from can be flagged for attention.

GUM INFLAMMATION (GINGIVITIS):
Signs of inflamed, swollen, or reddened gum tissue are identified. Gum inflammation is the early, reversible stage of gum disease and is extremely common — affecting roughly half of all adults at any given time.

GUM RECESSION:
The AI can identify areas where the gum line has pulled back from the teeth, exposing root surfaces. Gum recession is a sign of either aggressive brushing or advancing gum disease, and catching it early matters because receded tissue does not grow back on its own.

PLAQUE BUILD-UP:
Visible plaque accumulation along the gum line and between teeth is flagged. Plaque that is not removed hardens into calculus (tartar) and is the root cause of both cavities and gum disease.

CRACKED TOOTH:
The AI flags visual signs of tooth fracture or cracking, which can lead to sensitivity, pain, and structural failure if left untreated.

LIMITATIONS:
The AI screening analyses external photos only. It cannot detect:
- Cavities between teeth that are only visible on X-ray
- Conditions below the gum line that require clinical probing
- Issues inside the tooth structure (e.g. abscesses that aren't yet visible)
- Any condition that requires tactile or clinical examination by a dentist

The AI report is a screening and risk-flagging tool, not a substitute for a full clinical examination. All findings should be reviewed by a dentist in person.

WHAT THE ORAL LINK REPORT CONTAINS:
The Oral Link AI report provides a structured summary of dental findings based on the photos you submitted. It typically includes:

- A list of detected or suspected conditions (e.g. cavities, plaque, gum recession)
- A risk level or severity classification for each finding
- Clear treatment guidance explaining what action is recommended
- Image-based results — the specific photos and regions the AI flagged

The report is designed to be patient-friendly: findings are explained in plain language so you can understand your oral health status without needing to interpret clinical terminology.

HOW TO USE YOUR REPORT:
The report is a starting point for your dental journey, not an endpoint. Here is what to do with your findings:

If the report shows LOW RISK findings: Continue your current oral hygiene routine. Consider booking a routine dental check-up within 6 to 12 months if you have not had one recently.

If the report shows MODERATE findings: Book an appointment with a partner dental clinic soon. Moderate findings — such as moderate plaque, early gum inflammation, or early signs of cavities — are treatable and reversible if addressed promptly. Delaying increases the risk of progression.

If the report shows ADVANCED or HIGH RISK findings: Book a dental appointment as soon as possible. Advanced findings such as significant gum recession, multiple cavities, or suspected cracked teeth need in-person clinical assessment and treatment. Do not wait.

If you have PAIN, SWELLING, or BLEEDING that is not improving: Do not rely on the AI report — contact a dentist directly or visit a dental clinic promptly. These are symptoms that require clinical evaluation, not just a screening report.

WHAT THE REPORT DOES NOT DO:
The report does not provide a binding diagnosis. It does not replace an in-person dental examination. The final diagnosis, treatment decision, and any clinical work are performed by a qualified dentist at a partner clinic.

[TOPIC]      Why oral health matters for your overall health — the oral-systemic link
[DOMAIN]     OralLink clinic information — health education
[KEYWORDS]   oral health and heart, gum disease and diabetes, oral health and brain, mouth and lungs, oral health pregnancy, gum disease systemic, why oral health matters, oral health overall health
[TRIGGERS]
  - Why does oral health matter?
  - Can gum disease affect my heart?

  - Can bad teeth cause other health problems?

CONTENT:

Oral health is directly connected to overall body health. Poor oral health — particularly untreated gum disease — is linked to a wide range of serious systemic conditions. This is why dental screening and preventive care matters beyond just keeping your teeth.

HEART AND BLOOD HEALTH:
Gum disease increases the risk of heart attacks and strokes. Bacteria from infected gum tissue can enter the bloodstream, trigger inflammation in blood vessels, and contribute to the formation of arterial plaques. People with periodontitis (advanced gum disease) have a significantly higher risk of cardiovascular events.

LUNG HEALTH:
Poor oral hygiene can cause respiratory infections including pneumonia. Bacteria from the mouth can be inhaled into the lungs — particularly in elderly or hospitalised patients — causing or worsening lung infections.

DIABETES CONTROL:
Gum disease makes it harder to control blood sugar levels. The relationship works in both directions: high blood sugar impairs the body's ability to fight gum infection, and gum infection in turn makes blood sugar harder to manage. Improving gum health can meaningfully improve diabetes control.

PREGNANCY RISKS:
Gum infections are linked to premature births and low birth-weight babies. Pregnant women with untreated periodontal disease have a higher risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes. Dental care during pregnancy is safe and important.

BRAIN AND MEMORY:
Chronic gum disease is associated with an increased risk of Alzheimer's disease and memory loss. Research suggests that bacteria associated with periodontal disease (Porphyromonas gingivalis) can enter the brain and contribute to neuroinflammation.

CHRONIC INFLAMMATION AND ARTHRITIS:
Conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis are linked to gum disease. Chronic oral inflammation contributes to systemic inflammatory burden, which can worsen autoimmune and inflammatory conditions.

HEART INFECTION RISK (ENDOCARDITIS):
Bacteria from the mouth can cause endocarditis — a serious infection of the inner lining of the heart chambers. This is particularly relevant for people with heart valve conditions or previous heart surgery.

PRACTICAL IMPLICATION:
Treating gum disease and maintaining good oral hygiene is not just about your teeth — it can directly lower your risk of serious systemic illness. Oral Link's early detection service is one practical way to catch oral disease before it reaches the stage where these systemic risks escalate.

CONTENT:

Oral Link's AI screening focuses on five common dental conditions. Here is a plain-language explanation of each.

DENTAL CAVITIES (TOOTH DECAY):
A cavity is a hole that forms in a tooth when plaque bacteria produce acid that dissolves the tooth's enamel. Cavities start small and painless, but if left untreated they grow deeper — eventually reaching the nerve inside the tooth, causing pain and requiring more complex treatment like a root canal or extraction. Early-stage cavities can often be treated with a simple filling; advanced cavities may require a crown or root canal.

GUM INFLAMMATION (GINGIVITIS):
Gingivitis is inflammation of the gum tissue caused by plaque build-up along the gum line. Signs include red, swollen, or tender gums, and gums that bleed when you brush or floss. Gingivitis is the early, reversible stage of gum disease — it can be fully resolved with improved brushing, interdental cleaning, and a professional clean. If left untreated it can progress to periodontitis, which causes permanent damage to the bone supporting the teeth.

GUM RECESSION:
Gum recession occurs when the gum tissue pulls back from the tooth, exposing the root surface below. This can be caused by aggressive brushing, gum disease, or genetic factors. Exposed root surfaces are more sensitive and more vulnerable to decay. Recession is not reversible on its own — tissue that has receded does not grow back — but progression can be stopped with correct technique and treatment. In significant cases, a gum graft can restore lost tissue.

PLAQUE BUILD-UP:
Plaque is a soft, sticky film of bacteria that continuously forms on tooth surfaces. If not removed by brushing and interdental cleaning within 24 to 72 hours, it hardens into calculus (tartar), which cannot be removed by brushing and requires professional scaling. Plaque and calculus are the root cause of both tooth decay and gum disease.

CRACKED TOOTH:
A cracked tooth can range from a hairline fracture causing occasional sensitivity to a full fracture running through the tooth. Cracks can be caused by teeth grinding (bruxism), biting on hard foods, or large existing fillings that weaken the tooth structure. A cracked tooth may require a crown to protect it; if the crack extends into the pulp (nerve), root canal treatment may be needed before the crown.

CONTENT:

Singapore faces significant oral health challenges across all age groups. These are the problems that Oral Link was designed to help address:

CHILDHOOD DECAY:
50 percent of children in Singapore have cavities by the time they reach kindergarten (approximately age 4 to 5). This represents one of the highest rates of early childhood dental decay in a developed nation.

ADULT NEGLECT:
Three in four adults in Singapore have untreated gum disease or tooth decay. The majority of dental check-ups among adults happen only when pain or an obvious problem is already present — meaning that preventive care is largely absent from most adults' healthcare routines.

ELDERLY CARE GAP:
There are virtually no dental programmes in nursing homes or elderly care facilities in Singapore. As Singaporeans age and spend longer in residential care, their oral health often deteriorates with no systematic screening or preventive intervention.

ECONOMIC BURDEN:
Oral diseases cost approximately US$710 billion globally per year. Singapore's dental healthcare costs are rising, and a significant portion of this cost is driven by late-stage treatment of conditions that could have been caught earlier.

LACK OF PREVENTION:
Most dental encounters in Singapore are reactive (emergency or pain-driven) rather than preventive. The absence of a national adult dental screening programme means there is no systematic way to catch problems early.

SYSTEMIC HEALTH RISKS:
Poor oral health increases the risk of heart disease, diabetes complications, dementia, and other systemic conditions (see the oral-systemic health chunk). This means untreated dental disease has a cost that extends well beyond the mouth.

SCREENING GAPS:
There is no structured preventive dental care programme between the School Dental Service (for school-age children) and senior dental programmes — leaving most working-age adults without any supported pathway to proactive dental care.

OVERLOADED PUBLIC SYSTEM:
Long wait times at polyclinics and public hospitals discourage regular check-ups. Many people who would like subsidised care cannot access it promptly.

TECHNOLOGY GAP:
Singapore has been noted to trail Hong Kong and other regional peers in the adoption of mobile dental technology for population-level screening.
